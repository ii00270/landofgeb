02  —  The thesis

Efficiency isn't a claim.
It's the architecture.

Most operations price work before they understand it, then spend the engagement discovering what they agreed to. The overrun was written into the quote. Bixxville inverts the order: understand the work, then derive the number from it. The price is an output, never an input.

Scope before price

The number follows the work.

No engagement is priced before it is decomposed. Every unit of work carries an hour estimate and an owner, and the price is what those add up to — not a guess dressed as a quote.

The refusal gate

We decline what the calendar can't hold.

If the work cannot fit the window, the network declines to quote and says so. A hard stop — not a rush fee. Not overridable by a client's willingness to pay more, or a member's willingness to absorb it.

Binary deliverables

Done, or not done.

Every deliverable is defined before work starts, with no partial credit and no ambiguity about what triggers payment. There is nothing to argue about at the end, because the argument happened at the beginning.

Arithmetic integrity

The books check themselves.

Every ID carries mod-97 check digits. Before any cycle closes, a casting-out-nines audit confirms member pay, reserve, and remainder sum exactly to gross. Sealed cycles are immutable.

Why this matters on a contract. Schedule slip and scope dispute are the two things that kill a job and a reputation. The refusal gate is designed to make the first one structurally unlikely, and binary deliverables are designed to make the second one impossible to have.

04  —  How a job runs

Intake to settlement.

The same six moves on every engagement, whether it's a federal task order or a kitchen renovation.

01
Intake
The goal is stated in the client's own words, along with the window it has to happen in.
02
Decomposition
The goal becomes a dependency graph. Leaves are the priced, estimated units of work; branches are pure containers that roll up and are never separately billed. No oversight multiple. No consumption surcharge.
03
The gate
Estimated work is measured against the available window. If the critical path alone can't fit, the network declines and says what it would take — more people, or more time.
04
Price
The sized work becomes the price, with the reserve funded off the top rather than added on afterward. The client sees one flat number, and the derivation behind it is available on request.
05
Execute & verify
Work is claimed by members at their tier. Each deliverable closes binary against the definition written at scoping. Communication runs inside the system, next to the work.
06
Settle
A four-stamp protocol closes the engagement. Before the cycle seals, the arithmetic audit confirms every figure reconciles to gross. Sealed cycles are immutable.
